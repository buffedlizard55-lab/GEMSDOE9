Why 0.1563, and what to do instead

Everything on this page is algebra from the published metric, plus a synthetic experiment that measures the shape of a submission. No claim here depends on a machine-learning result, because we do not have one yet.

1 · The metric collapses to two ratios

Problem description, § Performance metric — source

k(d) = max(1 − d/R, 0),  R = 300 m = 3 px at 100 m
TP_w = Σ_{g∈G} max_{x: d(x,g)≤R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))
FP_w = Σ_{x: p(x)>0} p(x)·[1 − max_{g∈G} k(d(x,g))]
FN_w = Σ_{g∈G} [1 − max_{x: d(x,g)≤R} p(x)·k(d(x,g))]

α = 0.2 (false positives), β = 0.8 (false negatives)
DTI = TP_w / (TP_w + α·FP_w + β·FN_w + ε)

TP_w and FN_w are maxima of the same quantity, so FN_w = N − TP_w exactly, with N = |G|. Substituting:

DTI = T / (0.2·T + 0.2·F + 0.8·N)        (EQ-1)
    = ρ / (0.2·ρ + 0.2·φ + 0.8)   with ρ = T/N, φ = F/N   (EQ-2)

Only two numbers matter, and neither is the absolute pixel count. Everything below follows from that. Implementation: src/gems/strategy.py, cross-checked against src/gems/metric.py in tests B1–B9.

2 · 0.1563 is the score of a catalogue copy

A submission that paints the known USGS/INGENIOUS catalogue has zero false-positive mass, because staff masked those pixels out of evaluation:

“Pixels corresponding to known USGS/INGENIOUS faults are masked / excluded from evaluation, so they do not count towards penalty terms.”
chrisk-dd, DrivenData Staff, 2026-09-16 · thread 11516

Set φ = 0 in EQ-2 and solve for ρ at each headline score:

This is a derivation, not a measurement It predicts that any two catalogue-derived submissions land within about 10⁻³ of each other, and that 0.3049 requires roughly 2.0× the recall a catalogue copy gets. The submissions themselves are not public, so this cannot be confirmed against the actual files — but it is falsifiable, and the synthetic experiment in §4 measures the same relationship -- see src/gems/metric.py, where the closed form and the measured value are asserted equal in the test suite.

What it means practically: the group has been submitting the catalogue. The 0.1563 ceiling is not bad luck or weak hyper-parameters. It is the exact value that architecture produces.

3 · Recall is worth about an order of magnitude more than precision

At the 0.1563 attractor, one extra unit of weighted recall is worth 31 units of false-positive mass. Every instinct that says “be conservative, be sparse, be precise” points the wrong way for this metric: a new fault you find is worth about 31 pixels you get wrong.

A mistake this page used to make An earlier draft concluded from EQ-3 that a hard 0/1 mask must beat a soft probability map — the kernel is a maximum, so half-confidence at the right place should earn half the credit and pay half the penalty, and 31 > 1. That is wrong, and the experiment above is what caught it. On exactly the same pixel set, a soft field beat a hard one in 19 of 20 cells. Both effects scale together: fractional mass lowers TP and lowers FP by the same factor, so which one wins depends on which term binds. At a recall-limited field hard wins; once φ dominates, spreading wins. Which regime you are in is a property of the real data, so it has to be measured on the holdout, not assumed.

4 · The free-catalogue-core lever

Masking zeroes a pixel's contribution to F only. The maxima in TP_w run over all pixels, masked or not. So a prediction placed exactly on a known catalogue fault costs nothing at all, and still collects true-positive credit for every withheld fault within 300 m of it. Two staff statements make this worth acting on:

  • Known-catalogue pixels are excluded from the penalty terms (11516).
  • “New fault” = any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS, and can include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system (11536).

Together: a large share of the scored pixels are inside existing fault zones, and the free core already reaches them. The submission shape that maximises P(Win) is therefore neither “predict faults” nor “predict new faults”:

p = 1 on every catalogue pixel                 free  (masked → no FP cost)
p = 1 on a thin, evidence-gated halo around them    paid  (this is where new geometry is)
p = 0 everywhere else

3 px of halo is 300 m — exactly the metric's support radius. Beyond that the halo reaches faults it cannot earn credit for and is pure cost. build_corridor() in src/gems/strategy.py implements it; the halo radius and paid budget are calibrated by sweep on a training fold, never on the leaderboard.

Read the two gaps separately. Core → blind halo is +0.0391 and belongs to the halo's geometry, not to any detector. Chance detector → useful detector, inside the same corridor, is +0.0793 and is the only part of this table attributable to detection at all. 5 independent phantoms, 2×2 blocks, configuration chosen on the training blocks and scored on the held-out block.

A second, selection-free sweep ranks the whole grid instead of a band. It loses to the free core in 17/20 cells, at every budget from 0.1% to 10% of the raster and every detector quality. Proximity to a mapped fault is what the 300 m kernel rewards; spreading the same pixel count uniformly over the region does not.

Reading: proximity gating is the lever, not the pixel count. The metric's 300 m kernel pays for a pixel only if a withheld fault is within 300 m of it, and withheld faults are overwhelmingly near mapped ones — so the same budget spent inside a band around the catalogue beats the same budget spread over the raster.

What this does not show It is synthetic. It prices the arithmetic of the submission shape against the published metric and masking rule. It says nothing about Nevada geology, and it must not be cited as evidence that any detector finds faults. The detector claims are on the hypotheses page and are labelled unvalidated.

6 · Honest limits of this strategy

  • The mask may not be pixel-identical to our raster. The scorer masks the organiser's rasterisation of the vector catalogue; we are given existing_faults.tif. A one-pixel disagreement makes a supposedly-free pixel cost 1.0 of FP mass. build_corridor(mask_safety_px=…) exists for this; the safe default is 0 and the risk is unquantified until we can compare.
  • The halo has to be evidence-gated, not uniform. A 3 px dilation everywhere spends the whole FP budget on empty ground. The budget sweep in §5 has an interior optimum precisely because of this.
  • Phase 1 and Phase 2 reward different things. Phase 1 is a fixed private label set; Phase 2 adds whatever experts verify from everyone's submissions. A high-recall submission that is a superset of the Phase 1 truth converts into Phase 2 label additions. Do not over-tune to the public board.
  • The algebra cannot manufacture recall. EQ-2 tells you the price of recall; it does not find faults. Detector G-1 through G-5 are the part that has to work, and they are unvalidated.
