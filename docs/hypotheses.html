H-1 · Seismogenic organisation unvalidated on real data

Layers
density of earthquakes; shear strain rate (both listed in the official feature list, both previously unread by any detector in this repo)
Physical signature
earthquake-density structure-tensor coherence x shear strain rate, catalogue suppressed
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
The catalogue is a geomorphic map: it maps scarps. A fault that is slipping now but has no preserved scarp is invisible to it. Seismicity is the one shipped layer that measures present activity rather than past surface expression.
How it differs from anything already in this repo
No detector in this repo, G-1..G-5, reads earthquake density or shear strain rate. Verified by scripts/audit_layer_usage.py.

H-2 · Strike-integrated scarp step (cross-profile asymmetry) unvalidated on real data

Layers
detrended elevation
Physical signature
max over 8 azimuths of |&lt;|Dn f|&gt;_(+2px) - &lt;|Dn f|&gt;_(-2px)| after anisotropic along-strike smoothing (400 m along, 100 m across)
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
A scarp is the direct surface expression of a young normal fault. The catalogue-gap class the organisers define -- a continuation past a mapped tip, a splay, a parallel strand -- is precisely a scarp nobody has traced yet.
How it differs from anything already in this repo
G-5 is an ALONG-strike knickpoint detector. This is a CROSS-strike step/asymmetry detector. Different operator, different failure mode. Along-strike integration is what neither G-5 nor a plain gradient does.
Implementation cost
Low - one layer already in training_features.tif
Expected value
Highest per unit cost: no external data, one layer, and it targets the dominant fault type in the region. NEITHER IS VALIDATED ON REAL DATA. Ranked by expected value per unit of implementation cost, on the reasoning in the docstring, not on measurement.

H-3 · Gravity-gradient lineament unvalidated on real data

Layers
isostatic gravity anomaly; slope of the isostatic gravity anomaly (both listed in the official feature list, both previously unread)
Physical signature
horizontal-gradient magnitude of the isostatic anomaly, gated by the shipped gravity-slope layer and by along-strike gradient-azimuth coherence
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
Gravity sees through basin fill. Blind faults under the valleys have no scarp and no aerial expression, so a geomorphic catalogue cannot contain them; their density offset survives.
How it differs from anything already in this repo
G-4 is magnetics. Density and magnetisation decorrelate in the Great Basin (much of the basement is non-magnetic granite), so the two are not redundant.
Implementation cost
Low - both layers already in training_features.tif
Expected value
Medium. Isostatic anomalies are long-wavelength, so the achievable spatial precision at 100 m is the open question. NEITHER IS VALIDATED ON REAL DATA. Ranked by expected value per unit of implementation cost, on the reasoning in the docstring, not on measurement.

H-4 · Multi-depth conductance persistence blocked — needs external data

Layers
INGENIOUS electrical conductance, five depth ranges 2-200 km (NOT in training_features.tif)
Physical signature
resultant length of the doubled conductance-gradient azimuth averaged over depth slices, x mean gradient magnitude
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
Blind faults under basin fill. Depth persistence is what suppresses G-3&#x27;s admitted dominant false positive -- any lithologic contact makes a conductivity edge.
How it differs from anything already in this repo
G-3 uses one surface layer and a log-ratio. This uses a depth stack and a cross-depth azimuth statistic. It is the fix for G-3&#x27;s failure mode, not a variant of it.

H-5 · Hydrothermal expression alignment blocked — needs external data

Layers
INGENIOUS paleo-geothermal features (sinter, tufa) and Quaternary volcanic vents (NOT in training_features.tif)
Physical signature
point-lineament detector: best of 12 azimuths of the count of expression points aligned through a pixel within 1.5 px, normalised by local point density
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
A thermal spring or sinter deposit is direct evidence of fault-controlled fluid upflow and can exist where the controlling fault has no surface trace at all.
How it differs from anything already in this repo
Nothing in this repo uses hydrothermal surface-expression data. It is also the only candidate whose evidence is the geothermal system itself rather than a proxy for structure.
Implementation cost
Medium + a one-time external download (the files are small: 82 kB and 9.4 MB)
Expected value
Medium-High and the most sponsor-aligned, but blocked on data. NEITHER IS VALIDATED ON REAL DATA. Ranked by expected value per unit of implementation cost, on the reasoning in the docstring, not on measurement.

VerdictNO CANDIDATE CLEARS THE BAR. The best result is +0.053 AUC over proximity on 4 of 5 phantoms, and the second best is +0.029. Both are small relative to the spread the phantoms themselves produce -- one seed on H-1 is -0.092 -- and the number moved by 0.014 when a numerical defect in the harness was fixed, which is a direct measure of how little separates these candidates. No submission slot should be spent on any of them on the strength of this experiment.
The headline findingH-1 looked like the clear winner on the selection phantom (+0.102 AUC) and collapsed to +0.029 on unseen ones, while H-2 -- the top candidate by a-priori reasoning -- is negative on both. The ranking does not survive contact with a second phantom. That is the protocol working, not failing.
What this cannot showThe phantom's physics are OUR forward model. H-1 wins partly because seismicity was generated as a fault-parallel ribbon, and H-3 partly because every fault was given a gravity step. Both are the most favourable possible construction for those detectors. Decoys were included so a win means something, but a win here is necessary and never sufficient. Nothing in this output is evidence about the GeoDAWN region.

G-1 · Catalogue geometry completion unvalidated — needs data/

Layers
existing_faults.tif (as geometry); optional detrended-elevation slope + magnetic HGM modulation
Physical signature
endpoint proximity x local skeleton curvature x azimuthal deviation, gated to &lt;=3 px (300 m = the metric kernel support) outside the catalogue
Why it should catch a fault the catalogue misses
Staff have stated that &#x27;new fault&#x27; means any fault pixel not already captured by USGS/INGENIOUS and CAN include newly mapped geometry of an existing fault system. Continuations past mapped tips, splays and parallel strands are therefore both (a) common in the scored set and (b) located exactly where the catalogue says to look. Every sibling submission treated the catalogue as a target to copy instead of as a pointer.

G-4 · Magnetic contact edge unvalidated — needs data/

Layers

Physical signature

G-5 · Fluvial knickpoint and drainage deflection unvalidated — needs data/

Layers

GEMSDOE3 (three submissions)Sparse point placements — 'Pindrop' nodes, a catalogue-gap target, a dense ridge controlThese are point/line hypotheses with no continuous probability field. G-1 is a density field over a 300 m corridor; G-2..G-5 are continuous geophysical scores.
GEMSDOE4Lineament features plus proxy labels; scored 0.0343G-5 is the sharpened version of the lineament idea (knickpoint second derivatives plus drainage-azimuth circular variance on detrended elevation) and G-4 adds the tilt-zero gating that separates contacts from faults.
6GEMSDOEGradient boosting over 88 channels, top 3%; scored 0.0286Same model family, opposite failure: thresholding 3% of pixels with no geological prior puts almost all of the budget in empty ground. The metric algebra says precision is the cheap thing to buy and recall is the expensive one; 6GEMSDOE bought the wrong one.

G-1 is new and rests on an official statement the previous revision never cited. G-5 keeps the fluvial idea but runs on the competition's own detrended-elevation layers, so it is not blocked on 3DEP.

Validation protocol — fixed before any slot is spent

  1. Spatially-blocked holdout. 4×4 blocks with a 300 m buffer, so the classifier cannot learn a fault and be scored on the pixels touching it. Random pixel splits are meaningless here: catalogue strands are 1–2 px wide.
  2. Budget-matched random control at every arm, so "it scored well" is never confused with "it found something".
  3. Catalogue-only arm, because a model that merely reproduces the catalogue scores perfectly on catalogue labels and 0.1563 on the board.
  4. Gate: no submission file is written unless the corridor arm beats catalogue-only by ≥ 0.02 and the model arm beats random by ≥ 0.02. python scripts/validate_holdout.py exits non-zero and build_submission.py --holdout-gate refuses to write.
The limitation we cannot engineer away This holdout scores catalogue faults. The competition scores faults that are not in the catalogue. No public holdout can measure the thing we are scored on, because the labels do not exist publicly. The protocol above can prove a detector carries information beyond “where the catalogue already is”. It cannot prove the detector finds unmapped faults. That gap is the honest state of play, and it is why the metric algebra in Strategy — which is fully determined — carries more of the expected lift than any detector claim here.
