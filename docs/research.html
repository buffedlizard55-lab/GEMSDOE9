Geothermal Systems in Great Basin — Overview

Source: About page and USGS GeoDAWN DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ (Glen & Earney 2024).

Geothermal resources in northwestern Nevada are controlled by faults in Walker Lane and western Great Basin. Faults act as conduits for hot fluids. Hidden geothermal prospects lack surface hot springs but have blind faults with subtle geophysical expression.

From the official about page (image caption, verified verbatim 2026-09-27): “Geothermal resources are often found in regions with active or recent faulting, where fractures in the Earth's crust provide pathways for hot fluids to reach the surface.” — drivendata.org …/page/968/

About Page — Verified Facts and Official Literature Pointers (2026-09-27)

  • GeoDAWN = USGS + DOE high-resolution airborne magnetic and radiometric surveys over northern/western Nevada and eastern California (Walker Lane and western Great Basin), acquired under the USGS Earth Mapping Resources Initiative (EarthMRI) with DOE Office of Geothermal support.
  • Airborne lidar was collected coordinated with GeoDAWN through USGS 3DEP, “over a similar extent spanned by the geophysical surveys” — i.e., high-resolution topography exists for (much of) the study area. This is the official basis for H9-3 (fluvial/scarp detection from 1 m 3DEP DEMs).
  • “Most faults in the GeoDAWN region of Nevada are more subtle, and many are hidden below the surface, requiring geophysical data to detect.” (official about page caption)

Structural Controls — Verified Percentages (GDR 383)

Key literature (verified 2026-09-27, free official sources, links for manual review):

  • Faulds, Hinz & Kreemer — GDR submission 383, “Structural and Tectonic Controls of Geothermal Activity in the Basin and Range Province”: “Of the 250+ geothermal fields catalogued, step-overs or relay ramps in normal fault zones serve as the most favorable setting, hosting ~32% of the systems.”
  • DOE project DE-EE0002748, PI James Faulds (UNR), GBCGE “Characterizing Structural Controls”: step-overs/relay ramps ~32%, normal fault terminations 25%, intersections of normal and strike-slip/oblique faults 22%, accommodation zones 9%, displacement transfer zones 5%, pull-aparts 3%, bends 2%, major range-front faults 1%; ~21% of fields are hybrid/compound (e.g., Steamboat, Coso, Dixie Valley).
  • Faulds et al. 2012 New Zealand Geothermal Workshop paper (open PDF on GDR): gdr.openei.org/files/383/Faulds et al 2012 GeoNZ Paper.pdf — also notes most Great Basin geothermal resources are estimated to be blind/hidden, with no surface manifestations.
  • OSTI record 1724082, “Favorable tectonic and structural settings of geothermal systems in the Great Basin region, western USA: Proxies for discovering blind geothermal systems”: osti.gov/biblio/1724082 — same ~32% stepover figure; geothermal systems rare along major range-front faults (clay gouge, stress release).
  • Cumming 2009 clay-cap concept (conductive smectite cap over resistive reservoir) underlies H9-2; cited as a conceptual model — the specific percentage-free statement used here is the geometry of cap edge at fault, standard in MT interpretation of Great Basin systems (INGENIOUS MT conductance layers, DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU).

Correction note (2026-09-27): earlier text in this repo said “70% of Great Basin geothermal at stepovers (Faulds et al. 2010)”. That figure was not supported by any citable source and has been replaced with the verified breakdown above. The three interaction settings (stepovers + terminations + intersections) together account for ~79% of catalogued fields by simple sum, with hybrids noted.

Relay stepover: left-stepping en echelon faults in right-lateral Walker Lane create extensional jog, dilation, permeability spike 10-100×. Surface expression subtle (1-2 m scarp or none), but strain rate shows dilation.

Why USGS catalogue misses them: catalogue mapped from aerial photos and field scarp, misses short linking faults inside stepovers (<2 km) with no clear scarp but strong dilation. INGENIOUS compilation includes some blind but not all.

Walker Lane Tectonics — Verified

Source: USGS ScienceBase GeoDAWN area map and about page.

GeoDAWN covers northwestern Nevada and eastern California, Walker Lane and western Great Basin. Walker Lane is ~100 km wide dextral shear zone, accommodating ~20% of Pacific-North America plate motion. Right-lateral + extension → transtension, ideal for geothermal.

Fault types: normal (vertical displacement), reverse, strike-slip (horizontal) — diagram from USGS via Wikimedia Commons Fault_types.svg (verified).

Steamboat geothermal plants near Reno (image public domain via Wikimedia Steamboat_Geothermal_Plants,_Nevada.jpg) — within GeoDAWN study area, at fault stepover.

Why Blind Faults Are Missing From Catalogue

  • USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database: mapped from field scarp, aerial photos, lidar where available. Requires surface expression. Blind faults have no scarp, only subtle drainage or conductivity edge → missed.
  • INGENIOUS Great Basin Regional Dataset Compilation: DOI 10.15121/1881483 — compiles existing, but still incomplete for hidden geothermal.
  • Expert new faults (test set): manually labeled by NLR/USGS experts using GeoDAWN magnetic, radiometric, lidar, and field checks — these are the faults with subtle signatures that catalogue missed. That's why test set is harder.
  • Metric: distance-weighted Tversky with 300m kernel accounts for mis-alignment and rasterization lossy, but still rewards detection of subtle traces.

Free Official External Data — What Others Overlook

Competition allows external data if licensed (Rules § Use of external data). We list only free official:

  • USGS 3DEP 10m DEM (1/3 arcsec): https://apps.nationalmap.gov/3dep/ — seamless, 100% coverage, 9 topo bands derived in 8GEMSDOE, verified. Free official.
  • USGS GeoDAWN radiometric: DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ — 7 bands K, Th, U, TC, U/K, Th/K, U/Th — 99.975% coverage, verified in 8GEMSDOE. Free official.
  • Landsat-8/9 TIRS thermal: EarthExplorer — Band 10-11 100m thermal, free official, for H9-4 thermal anomaly.
  • ASTER L1T: Earthdata Search — clay alteration indices kaolinite, alunite, silica — free official NASA/USGS.
  • USGS 3DEP 1m DEM tiles: 716 URLs in 1m_DEM_links.csv, via https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/?list-type=2&prefix=StagedProducts/Elevation/1m/ — one verified live 185,344,605 B — ~130 GB total — free official but heavy.
