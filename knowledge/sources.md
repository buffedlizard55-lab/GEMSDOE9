## Scientific interpretation: where a fault is not enough

- [USGS Publications Warehouse: Southern Gabbs Valley blind geothermal system (Faulds et al., 2018)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70201803) describes an intersection/transfer zone revealed by gravity, magnetic and MT data, supported by a shallow temperature anomaly and warm gradient wells, **without surface hot springs or fumaroles**. This is a case study, not proof that our raster predicts similar systems. Lesson: geothermal interpretation requires independent heat/fluid evidence, whereas GEMS scores fault pixels.
- [USGS Publications Warehouse: Siler (2023)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70246663) reports hydrothermal settings at fault interactions, with especially localized modeled stress/strain at stepovers and terminations. This supports considering structural geometry, but is not a guaranteed per-pixel weighting for the competition and does not vindicate the legacy H9 builder without a holdout.
- [USGS Publications Warehouse: Grizzly Valley fault (2012)](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70045110) reports airborne LiDAR combined with seismic reflection to interpret a steep fault offsetting volcanic basement and basin fill. That is a location-specific example of why #4's offset/drainage idea needs both high-resolution surface data and independent checks; 100 m pixels cannot resolve its smallest offsets.
- [DOE Hidden Systems](https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/hidden-systems) explicitly distinguishes subsurface resources from obvious hot springs and says 3D geologic, gravity, reflection seismic and MT imaging work best when coupled with geological/geochemical studies. Therefore a gravity-only line is insufficient to assert a geothermal vent.

**Tag discrepancy:** problem page describes “depth to conductive base surface” but band 15 actually tags `depth_to_base_surf` (depth to basement). The program uses the TIFF tag and does **not** claim to model the conductive base. Sibling reports question band 6 `tc`; G9 avoids it. Physical sign convention for depth is inferred from the tag and was not cross-checked against a geologic map. Sensitivity to the opposite sign is a future experiment, not a reason to tune this test after seeing its score.
